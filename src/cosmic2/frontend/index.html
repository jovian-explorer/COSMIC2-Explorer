Welcome to COSMIC-2 Explorer: Step-by-Step Workflow Guide

Learn how to ingest, filter, condition, compare, and export GNSS Radio Occultation (GNSS-RO) soundings from the six-satellite FORMOSAT-7 / COSMIC-2 constellation, which samples about 45°S to 45°N.

Developed by Keshav Aggarwal, Research Associate at Space Physics Laboratory (SPL), Vikram Sarabhai Space Centre (VSSC), Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), Thiruvananthapuram, Kerala, India (Former PMRF Scholar at DAASE, IIT Indore).

Instant offline exploration with real occultation files.

Step 1

Loading and Ingesting GNSS-RO Data

COSMIC-2 data is archived by the University Corporation for Atmospheric Research (UCAR) COSMIC Data Analysis and Archive Center (CDAAC). Data is categorized into distinct publication streams:

  • Near Real-Time (nrt): produced within about 30-120 minutes of observation, with predicted orbits and clocks; used by weather centres.
  • Rapid: reprocessed within days with better orbits; the scintillation (scnLv2) and in-situ IVM (ivmL2m) files are here; ionospheric profiles (ionPrf) are in the provisional stream.
  • Provisional (space weather): provisional space-weather release, including the ionospheric electron-density profiles (ionPrf).
  • Post-Processed (postProc): reprocessing with final orbits and a frozen software version. For COSMIC-2, CDAAC holds only the in-situ IVM files here (ivmL2m, 2019-08-01 to 2022-05-31) and podTc2 of 2020; there is no post-processed atmPrf, wetPf2 or ionPrf, so the radio occultation profiles come from nrt.
  • Bundled examples: 12 real granules (atmPrf, wetPf2, echPrf, avnPrf, ionPrf, ivmL2m, scnLv2) ship with the app, so every feature can be tried without a network.
  • Drag-and-Drop Ingestion: Drag any .nc or _nc files from your desktop directly into this window to index them on the fly.
Step 2

Exploring Spatial Soundings

Every dot is the location of one occultation, where a GPS or GLONASS signal crossed the atmosphere:

  • Interactive Map: Visualize occultation distributions on an offline TopoJSON map. Color points by local solar time, latitude, or vertical sampling levels.
  • Spatial Bounding Boxes: Filter profiles using latitude and longitude limits.
  • Quick Latitude Presets: All Tropics (30°S - 30°N), Equatorial (10°S - 10°N), Northern Tropics (0° - 30°N), Southern Tropics (30°S - 0°), or Global.
  • Local Solar Time (LST): Constrain soundings to specific diurnal cycles (0 to 24 h) to isolate daytime convection or nocturnal cooling.
  • Quality Control (QC): "Good quality only" leaves out profiles CDAAC flagged as bad.
Step 3

Vertical Profile Inspection

Draw one or many profiles of any quantity against height: neutral profiles reach 60 km (wetPf2) or 80 km (atmPrf), ionospheric ones the satellite's orbit:

  • Thermodynamic Variables: Temperature (wetPf2 Temp, or the dry temperature of atmPrf Temp and wetPf2 temp_dry), pressure (Pres), refractivity (Ref, ref) and bending angle, plus derived fields: potential temperature, lapse rate, N², density, scale height and others.
  • Atmospheric Moisture: Water-vapour pressure (Vp), specific humidity (sph) and relative humidity (rh) from CDAAC's 1D-Var retrieval in wetPf2.
  • Ionospheric Soundings: Electron density (ELEC_dens) profiles from ionPrf, up to the satellite's orbit (about 550-580 km); S4 scintillation (scnLv2) and in-situ ion density (ivmL2m).
  • Multi-Profile Overlay: Select multiple occultations from the Explore table or lasso-select points on the map to compare soundings on a single vertical axis.
  • Diagnostics: WMO and cold-point tropopause, boundary-layer height, the steepest refractivity gradient, column water vapour, gravity-wave energy, F2 peak, foF2 and TEC, each cross-checked against CDAAC's own value where the file has one.
Smith-Weintraub Refractivity Formulation:

$$N = 77.6 \frac{P}{T} + 3.73 \times 10^5 \frac{e}{T^2} - 40.3 \times 10^6 \frac{N_e}{f^2}$$

Hydrostatic Neutral Dry Temperature:

$$T_{dry}(z) = \frac{\bar{m}}{k_B \cdot N(z)} \int_z^\infty g(z') N(z')\,dz'$$

Step 4

Applying Mathematical Pipeline Conditioning

Clean raw measurements and suppress high-frequency instrument noise using configurable statistical conditioning filters:

  • Outlier Removal: Filter noisy spikes using Sigma Clipping (Gaussian assumption), MAD Robust Filtering (Median Absolute Deviation), IQR Filter, or Percentile Truncation.
  • Smoothing Algorithms: Apply Savitzky-Golay polynomial smoothing (preserves peak shapes, tropopause sharp gradients, and structural features), Gaussian kernel filtering, or Rolling Moving Averages.
  • Uncertainty Envelopes: Generate rolling standard deviation or standard error bands along the profile, computed about the running mean so the vertical gradient is not mistaken for noise.
  • On screen only: these settings shape the plot; the caption lists what was applied, and exports keep the unfiltered archive values (their provenance records the settings as not applied).
Poisson Potential Temperature ($\theta$):

$$\theta(z) = T(z) \left(\frac{P_0}{P(z)}\right)^{\frac{R_{dry}}{C_p}}$$

Brunt-Vaisala Static Stability ($N_{BV}^2$):

$$N_{BV}^2(z) = \frac{g_0}{T(z)}\left(\frac{dT}{dz} + \Gamma_d\right) \approx \frac{g_0}{\theta(z)}\frac{d\theta}{dz}$$

Step 5

Multi-Profile Comparative Analysis

Transition from individual sounding weather snapshots to climate-scale regional statistics:

  • Grouped averages: average profiles by latitude band (equatorial 10°S-10°N, subtropics 10-30°, mid-latitudes 30-60°), hemisphere, day or night (06-18 h local solar time), date, product or satellite, on a common height grid.
  • Statistical Spread: Display group mean profiles with standard deviation (±1 sigma) envelopes, 10th-90th percentile ranges, or the 95% confidence interval of the mean (Student's t; profiles taken as independent, sampling error not included). An optional robust screen leaves out profiles far from their group's median over at least 1 km.
  • Derived Relationship Scatter: plot any per-profile diagnostic against another (tropopause height against latitude, foF2 against local time, gravity-wave energy against latitude), coloured by a third.
Step 5b

Statistics, Climatology and Validation

The Statistics tab works on the whole Explore search (or your selection):

  • Sampling and quality: counts by product, latitude and local time, CDAAC's bad flags, and penetration depth (lowest level reached, above sea level and above the ground).
  • Latitude-height sections: zonal means of temperature, refractivity, electron density or any other quantity, or each band's departure from the mean of all profiles.
  • Maps of a diagnostic: gravity-wave energy (any layer, polynomial or high-pass background), tropopause height, foF2 and more, in latitude-longitude cells with the zonal mean.
  • atmPrf and wetPf2 of the same occultations: the difference between the observed (dry) retrieval and the 1D-Var moist retrieval made from it, occultation by occultation: refractivity, dry temperature and pressure, and how much colder the dry temperature is than the real one (the moisture effect), with spread and 95% confidence interval by height.
  • Tropopause and cold point by latitude: the WMO lapse-rate tropopause and the cold point in each latitude band, with the 95% confidence interval of the mean, beside CDAAC's own tropopause values when atmPrf is selected.
  • Weather models at the same occultations: RO minus the ECMWF and NCEP profiles CDAAC colocates with each occultation (refractivity, temperature, and bending angle against ECMWF by impact height), the refractivity bias below the ducts ECMWF diagnoses, and the three-cornered hat, which estimates the refractivity error of each of the three from the spread of their differences, with bootstrap 95% intervals.
  • COSMIC-2 and other missions: COSMIC-2 against PAZ or KOMPSAT-5 (download them through their streams on the Get data tab), from collocated pairs (also less ECMWF's own difference between the two occultations) and from the double difference through ECMWF over the latitudes both cover, with 95% intervals. With another mission on disk, choose "COSMIC-2, all satellites" in the Explore satellite filter to keep it out of the other statistics.
  • Radiosonde comparison: each profile against the nearest NOAA IGRA v2 radiosonde within a distance and time window, as mean and spread of the temperature, refractivity and humidity differences by height; with atmPrf, also the boundary-layer tops of both, occultation against sonde.
Step 6

Exporting Figures and Data

Export your results in standard formats suitable for peer-reviewed journal articles, technical reports, and scientific archives:

  • Publication Figures: Matplotlib figures in PNG (300 dpi by default), PDF, SVG or EPS.
  • NetCDF-4: profiles regridded to a common height grid (profile × height arrays) with CF-style attributes, UDUNITS units and the provenance as global attributes.
  • Tabular Data (CSV): Level-by-level sounding profiles, uniform altitude grid matrices, or metadata catalog tables.
  • Provenance and Citations: data files carry the input file names, software version and the UCAR data citation; BibTeX entries for the data set and this software are given below.

Mathematical Formulations of the GNSS-RO Processing Pipeline

Atmospheric Physics & Retrieval Chain

The retrieval chain that produces the files, from phase delay to thermodynamic profiles. Steps 1-6 are performed by UCAR CDAAC before the data reach this app; steps 7-8 are computed here from the profiles.

1. Doppler Shift & Geometric Optical Impact Parameter

The relative motion of the transmitter and receiver imposes an atmospheric excess Doppler shift \( \Delta f_D \). Under spherical symmetry, Bouguer's law relates the impact parameter \( a \) to local refractive index \( n \) and radius \( r \):

$$a = n(r) \cdot r \cdot \sin\phi$$

Reference: Kursinski et al. (1997), J. Geophys. Res., 102(D19), 23429-23465. DOI: 10.1029/97JD01569

2. Dual-Frequency Ionospheric Combination

First-order dispersive ionospheric bending is eliminated by taking the linear combination of bending angles \( \alpha_1 \) and \( \alpha_2 \) measured on frequencies \( f_1 \) and \( f_2 \) (COSMIC-2: GPS L1 1575.42 and L2C 1227.60 MHz, Galileo E1 1575.42 and E5a 1176.45 MHz, GLONASS L1 and L2 near 1602 and 1246 MHz, each satellite on its own channel; the atmPrf attributes freq1 and freq2 name the signals):

$$\alpha_{corr}(a) = \frac{f_1^2 \alpha_1(a) - f_2^2 \alpha_2(a)}{f_1^2 - f_2^2}$$

Reference: Vorob'ev & Krasil'nikova (1994), Izvestiya Atmos. Ocean. Phys., 29, 602-609.

3. Abel Transform Inversion for Refractivity

Assuming local spherical symmetry in the neutral atmosphere, the integral equation for ray bending angle is inverted analytically via the Abel integral transform to compute refractive index \( n(r) \):

$$n(r) = \exp\left[ \frac{1}{\pi} \int_a^\infty \frac{\alpha(x)}{\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}} \, dx \right], \quad N = (n - 1) \times 10^6$$

Reference: Fjeldbo, Kliore & Eshleman (1971), Astron. J., 76, 123-140. DOI: 10.1086/111096

4. Smith-Weintraub Refractivity Decomposition

At radio frequencies, refractivity \( N \) depends on dry atmospheric constituents, dipolar water vapor polarization, and free electrons:

$$N = 77.6 \frac{P}{T} + 3.73 \times 10^5 \frac{e}{T^2} - 40.3 \times 10^6 \frac{N_e}{f^2}$$

Reference: Smith & Weintraub (1953), Proc. IRE, 41(8), 1035-1037. DOI: 10.1109/JRPROC.1953.274297

5. Hydrostatic Integration for Dry Temperature

Assuming dry air (\( e \approx 0 \)), density is proportional to refractivity (\( \rho \propto N \)). Combining the hydrostatic equation \( dP = -\rho g dz \) and the ideal gas law gives dry temperature by downward integration:

$$T_{dry}(z) = \frac{\bar{m}_{dry}}{k_B \cdot N(z)} \int_z^\infty g(z') N(z') \, dz'$$

Reference: Kursinski et al. (1997), J. Geophys. Res., 102(D19), 23429-23465. DOI: 10.1029/97JD01569

6. 1D-Var Optimal Estimation (Moisture Separation)

In the warm, moist lower troposphere, CDAAC separates temperature and water vapour (wetPf2) by minimising the 1D-Var cost function \( J(\mathbf{x}) \) with a numerical weather prediction background state:

$$J(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_b)^T \mathbf{B}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_b) + \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{y} - H(\mathbf{x}))^T \mathbf{R}^{-1} (\mathbf{y} - H(\mathbf{x}))$$

Reference: Rodgers (2000), Inverse Methods for Atmospheric Sounding; Healy & Eyre (2000), QJRMS, 126, 1661-1683.

7. Static Stability & Brunt-Vaisala Frequency (\( N_{BV}^2 \))

Atmospheric resistance to vertical displacement is governed by the squared buoyancy frequency \( N_{BV}^2 \). Negative values denote convective instability; strong peaks indicate the tropopause inversion layer (TIL):

$$N_{BV}^2(z) = \frac{g_0}{T(z)} \left( \frac{dT}{dz} + \frac{g_0}{c_p} \right) \approx \frac{g_0}{\theta(z)} \frac{d\theta}{dz}$$

Reference: Vaisala (1925), Soc. Sci. Fenn. Commentat. Phys.-Math., 2(19); Brunt (1927), QJRMS, 53, 30-32.

8. Lower Stratospheric Gravity Wave Potential Energy (\( E_p \))

Computed here for every neutral profile: the temperature between 20 and 40 km is detrended with a 4th-order polynomial in height (the background \( T_0 \), which also gives \( N_{BV} \)) to isolate the fluctuations \( T' = T - T_0 \), and \( E_p \) is averaged over the layer:

$$E_p = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{g}{N_{BV}}\right)^2 \left(\frac{T'}{T_0}\right)^2 \quad [\mathrm{J/kg}]$$

Reference: Tsuda et al. (2000), J. Geophys. Res., 105(D6), 7257-7273. DOI: 10.1029/1999JD901005

What is this? A GPS satellite's radio signal bends as it grazes Earth's atmosphere. Measuring that bend gives a vertical profile of temperature, pressure, humidity and electron density - like a weather balloon, but from orbit, several thousand times a day. Weather models at the same occultations (ECMWF, NCEP)

CDAAC colocates the ECMWF fields (echPrf) and the NCEP fields (avnPrf) with every occultation, named like its atmPrf; download them for the same days on the Get data tab (20-70 MB a day). The three-cornered hat estimates the error of each of the three from the variances of their differences.

COSMIC-2 and other missions (PAZ, KOMPSAT-5)

Download the other mission for the same days on the Get data tab (streams "PAZ near-real-time" and "KOMPSAT-5 near-real-time"), with its echPrf and COSMIC-2's for the comparison through ECMWF. The dates and filters of the Explore search apply.

Comparison with radiosondes (NOAA IGRA v2)

Each occultation is paired with the nearest radiosonde launched within the distance and time window; station files are downloaded from NOAA NCEI the first time (no account needed) and cached. Differences are occultation minus sonde on a common geopotential height.
