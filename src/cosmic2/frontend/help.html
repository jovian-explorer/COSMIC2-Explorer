COSMIC-2 Scientific Guidebook

Welcome to the COSMIC-2 Explorer Guidebook. This platform transforms raw GNSS radio occultation (RO) soundings into scientifically actionable physical datasets, designed for atmospheric research, space weather investigations, and meteorological education.

1. Mission and Constellation Overview

The FORMOSAT-7 / COSMIC-2 (Constellation Observing System for Meteorology, Ionosphere, and Climate - 2) mission is a joint program between the Taiwan Space Agency (TASA / NSPO) and the United States (NOAA, USAF). Launched on June 25, 2019, into a 24-degree inclination low Earth orbit (LEO) at ~550 km altitude, the constellation comprises six advanced microsatellites.

Each spacecraft carries the state-of-the-art TriG GNSS Radio Occultation System (TGRS) receiver developed by NASA JPL. The TGRS tracks multi-GNSS signals (GPS, GLONASS, and Galileo) with high-gain beam-steering antennas, capturing over 5,000 high-quality soundings per day across tropical and subtropical latitudes (30°S to 30°N).

2. GNSS Radio Occultation Fundamentals

Radio Occultation is an active limb-sounding technique utilizing the refraction of microwave signals passing through Earth's atmosphere:

  • Signal Geometry: As a transmitting GNSS satellite sets or rises across the atmospheric limb relative to a COSMIC-2 receiver, the radio wave traverses refractive gradients.
  • Phase Delay and Bending Angle: The atmospheric density gradient decelerates and curves the ray path. Precise dual-frequency carrier phase measurements yield the Doppler shift, which is converted to total bending angle $\alpha(a)$ as a function of impact parameter $a$.
  • Abel Inversion: Under spherical symmetry, the Abel transform inverts the bending angle profile to compute neutral atmospheric refractive index $\mu(r)$:

$$\mu(r) - 1 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_r^{r_{top}} \frac{\alpha(a)}{\sqrt{a^2 - r^2}}\,da$$

3. Physical Quantities and Governing Equations

A. Microwave Refractivity ($N$)

In the terrestrial neutral atmosphere and ionosphere, microwave refractivity $N = (\mu - 1) \times 10^6$ is governed by the Smith-Weintraub equation:

$$N = 77.6 \frac{P}{T} + 3.73 \times 10^5 \frac{e}{T^2} - 40.3 \times 10^6 \frac{N_e}{f^2}$$

where $P$ is total atmospheric pressure (hPa), $T$ is temperature (K), $e$ is water vapor partial pressure (hPa), $N_e$ is electron density ($\text{m}^{-3}$), and $f$ is operating signal frequency (Hz).

B. Hydrostatic Integration and Dry Temperature ($T_{dry}$)

Above ~10 km altitude where water vapor $e \approx 0$, refractivity is proportional to density $\rho \approx P / (R_{dry} T)$. Combining refractivity with the hydrostatic equation $\frac{dP}{dz} = -\rho(z) g(z)$ yields the dry temperature profile without external model priors:

$$T_{dry}(z) = \frac{\bar{m}}{k_B \cdot N(z)} \int_z^\infty g(z') N(z')\,dz'$$

C. Atmospheric Density ($\rho$) and Scale Height ($H$)

Thermodynamic mass density and local barometric scale height are evaluated through the ideal gas relation:

$$\rho(z) = \frac{P(z)}{R_{dry} T(z)}, \quad H(z) = \frac{R_{dry} T(z)}{g(z)}$$

where specific gas constant $R_{dry} = 287.058\text{ J}/(\text{kg}\cdot\text{K})$ and local gravity $g(z) = g_0 (R_E / (R_E + z))^2$.

D. Poisson Potential Temperature ($\theta$)

Quantifies the temperature an air parcel would attain if compressed adiabatically to the standard sea-level reference pressure $P_0 = 1013.25\text{ hPa}$:

$$\theta(z) = T(z) \left(\frac{P_0}{P(z)}\right)^{\frac{R_{dry}}{C_p}}$$

where isobaric specific heat capacity $C_p = 1004.67\text{ J}/(\text{kg}\cdot\text{K})$ and $R_{dry}/C_p \approx 0.2857$.

E. Brunt-Vaisala Static Stability Frequency ($N_{BV}^2$)

Measures buoyant oscillatory stability against vertical convective displacement:

$$N_{BV}^2(z) = \frac{g(z)}{T(z)}\left(\frac{dT}{dz} + \Gamma_d\right) = \frac{g(z)}{\theta(z)}\frac{d\theta}{dz}$$

where dry adiabatic lapse rate $\Gamma_d = g / C_p \approx 9.8\text{ K/km}$. Layers with $N_{BV}^2 > 0$ are stably stratified; layers with $N_{BV}^2 < 0$ indicate convective overturning instability.

F. Ionospheric Electron Density ($N_e$) and VTEC

From dual-frequency dispersive phase soundings (e.g. ionPrf products), vertical electron density $N_e(z)$ is extracted from ~80 km to 800 km altitude. Vertical Total Electron Content (VTEC) is derived by integrating plasma density:

$$\text{VTEC} = 10^{-7} \int_{z_{base}}^{z_{top}} N_e(z)\,dz \quad [\text{TECU}]$$

where $1\text{ TECU} = 10^{16}\text{ electrons/m}^2$.

4. Scientific Processing Pipeline

COSMIC-2 Explorer features an analytical pipeline enabling reproducible data conditioning:

  1. Quality Control Filtering: Restricts soundings to high-confidence retrievals passing CDAAC quality flags (bad == 0).
  2. Statistical Outlier Filtering:
    • Sigma Clipping: Iterative rejection beyond $N\sigma$ from local mean.
    • Median Absolute Deviation (MAD): Robust non-parametric rejection for non-Gaussian outliers.
    • Interquartile Range (IQR): Rejection of data falling outside $[Q_1 - 1.5\cdot\text{IQR}, Q_3 + 1.5\cdot\text{IQR}]$.
  3. Vertical Smoothing Filters:
    • Moving Average: Uniform boxcar window filter.
    • Gaussian Filter: Distance-weighted kernel preserving natural inflection points.
    • Savitzky-Golay Filter: Local polynomial regression preserving sharp tropopause gradients.
  4. Uncertainty Quantification: Computes running sample standard deviations and standard errors of the mean across profiles.
