COSMIC-2 Scientific Guidebook

COSMIC-2 Explorer finds, downloads, plots, compares and exports the radio occultation (RO) profiles that UCAR's COSMIC Data Analysis and Archive Center (CDAAC) publishes for FORMOSAT-7 / COSMIC-2. The retrieval itself (bending angle, refractivity, temperature, electron density) is done by CDAAC; this app reads those files and computes diagnostics from them (tropopause, gravity-wave energy, ionospheric peak, TEC, water-vapour column and others).

1. Mission and Constellation Overview

The FORMOSAT-7 / COSMIC-2 (Constellation Observing System for Meteorology, Ionosphere, and Climate - 2) mission is a joint program between the Taiwan Space Agency (TASA / NSPO) and the United States (NOAA, USAF). The six satellites were launched on 25 June 2019 into low Earth orbits inclined at 24 degrees, at about 550 km altitude.

Each spacecraft carries the TriG GNSS Radio Occultation System (TGRS) receiver developed by NASA JPL, which tracks GPS and GLONASS signals through high-gain antennas. The constellation produces several thousand neutral-atmosphere profiles a day between about 45°S and 45°N, densest in the tropics.

2. GNSS Radio Occultation Fundamentals (done by CDAAC)

Radio occultation is a limb-sounding technique that uses the refraction of GNSS signals passing through Earth's atmosphere:

  • Signal Geometry: As a GNSS satellite sets or rises behind the atmospheric limb as seen from a COSMIC-2 receiver, its signal crosses ever deeper layers of the atmosphere.
  • Phase Delay and Bending Angle: Refractivity gradients slow and bend the ray. Dual-frequency carrier-phase measurements give the Doppler shift, which is converted to the bending angle $\alpha(a)$ as a function of impact parameter $a$.
  • Abel Inversion: Under local spherical symmetry, the Abel transform inverts the bending-angle profile to the refractive index $\mu$ at radius $r$, where $a = \mu(r)\,r$:

$$\ln \mu(r) = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{a}^{\infty} \frac{\alpha(x)}{\sqrt{x^2 - a^2}}\,dx$$

3. Physical Quantities and Governing Equations

A. Microwave Refractivity ($N$)

Refractivity $N = (\mu - 1) \times 10^6$ follows the Smith-Weintraub relation (the ionospheric term is removed by CDAAC's dual-frequency combination before the neutral retrieval):

$$N = 77.6 \frac{P}{T} + 3.73 \times 10^5 \frac{e}{T^2} - 40.3 \times 10^6 \frac{N_e}{f^2}$$

where $P$ is total pressure (hPa), $T$ temperature (K), $e$ water-vapour pressure (hPa), $N_e$ electron density ($\text{m}^{-3}$) and $f$ the signal frequency (Hz).

B. Hydrostatic Integration and Dry Temperature ($T_{dry}$)

If water vapour is ignored ($e = 0$), refractivity is proportional to density. Combining it with the hydrostatic equation $dP/dz = -\rho g$ gives the dry temperature (atmPrf Temp), which needs no weather-model background (CDAAC initialises the integral at the top of the profile):

$$T_{dry}(z) = \frac{\bar{m}}{k_B \cdot N(z)} \int_z^\infty g(z') N(z')\,dz'$$

Dry temperature equals the real temperature only where the air is dry: above about 8-10 km in the tropics. Below that, use the 1D-Var moist temperature of wetPf2.

C. Atmospheric Density ($\rho$) and Scale Height ($H$)

Computed by this app at every level, with constant standard gravity $g_0 = 9.80665\ \text{m s}^{-2}$:

$$\rho(z) = \frac{P - e}{R_{dry} T} + \frac{e}{R_v T}, \quad H(z) = \frac{R_{dry} T(z)}{g_0}$$

with $R_{dry} = 287.058\ \text{J}/(\text{kg K})$ and $R_v = 461.5\ \text{J}/(\text{kg K})$. The vapour term uses wetPf2's Vp; atmPrf has no humidity, so $e = 0$ there.

D. Potential Temperature ($\theta$)

The temperature an air parcel would reach if brought adiabatically to the reference pressure $P_0 = 1000$ hPa:

$$\theta(z) = T(z) \left(\frac{P_0}{P(z)}\right)^{\frac{R_{dry}}{c_p}}$$

with $c_p = 1004.6\ \text{J}/(\text{kg K})$, so $R_{dry}/c_p \approx 0.2857$.

E. Brunt-Vaisala Frequency ($N_{BV}^2$)

Static stability against vertical displacement, with constant $g_0$:

$$N_{BV}^2(z) = \frac{g_0}{T(z)}\left(\frac{dT}{dz} + \Gamma_d\right) \approx \frac{g_0}{\theta(z)}\frac{d\theta}{dz}$$

where $\Gamma_d = g_0 / c_p \approx 9.8$ K/km. $N_{BV}^2 > 0$ is stably stratified; $N_{BV}^2 < 0$ is statically unstable.

F. Gravity-Wave Potential Energy ($E_p$)

For each neutral profile, the temperature between 20 and 40 km is put on a 0.2 km grid, a 4th-order polynomial in height is fitted as the background $T_0$, and the residual is the wave perturbation $T' = T - T_0$. Then

$$E_p = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{g_0}{N_{BV}}\right)^2 \left(\frac{T'}{T_0}\right)^2$$

is averaged over the layer, with $N_{BV}$ from $T_0$ (Tsuda et al., 2000). atmPrf uses dry temperature, which is the real temperature at these heights.

The polynomial filter (used for the per-profile diagnostic) keeps short waves and weakens long ones: for a test wave it recovers 98 % of the energy at 2 km vertical wavelength, 90 % at 5 km, 75 % at 10 km and 1 % at 20 km. The diagnostic map can instead use a zero-phase Butterworth high-pass with a chosen cut-off (10 km by default, the separation Tsuda et al. used), which keeps 98-99 % at 2-5 km, a quarter at the cut-off and under 1 % at 15 km. With the high-pass over 20-30 km, the 2026 sample's tropical (|lat| < 20°) mean is 6.0 J/kg, within the 6-10 J/kg Tsuda et al. reported for that layer.

G. Ionospheric Electron Density ($N_e$) and VTEC

CDAAC's ionPrf profiles run from the lower ionosphere up to the satellite's own orbit (about 550-580 km). This app integrates the density to vertical TEC:

$$\text{VTEC} = 10^{-7} \int_{z_{base}}^{z_{top}} \max(N_e, 0)\,dz \quad [\text{TECU}]$$

with $N_e$ in el/cm$^3$ and $z$ in km ($1\text{ TECU} = 10^{16}\text{ electrons/m}^2$). Negative densities, an Abel-inversion artefact of the bottomside, count as zero, as in CDAAC's own tec0. Both the column below hmF2 and the whole-profile column are reported.

H. Sampling, Quality and Penetration Depth (Statistics tab)

The Statistics tab counts the profiles of the current Explore search (or your selection) by product, latitude and local solar time, including those CDAAC flagged as bad, with their bad codes. The penetration depth is the height of the lowest retrieved level of each good atmPrf or wetPf2 profile above mean sea level. For atmPrf it is also given above the ground, using the terrain height stored in the wetPf2 file retrieved from the same occultation (both products of the day must be on disk). The atmPrf value is checked against CDAAC's own profile_bottom_km.

I. Latitude-Height Sections (Statistics tab)

Every profile of one product is put on a common height grid as for the Compare tab (log interpolation for pressure, density and electron density, never across gaps wider than 2 km) and averaged in latitude bands. A cell with fewer than the chosen minimum of profiles is left blank. Besides the mean, spread and count, the section can show each band's departure from the mean of all profiles at the same height:

$$\Delta(\phi, z) = 100\,\frac{\bar{x}_{\phi}(z) - \bar{x}(z)}{\bar{x}(z)}\ \%$$

For refractivity this removes N's exponential fall-off with height and leaves its latitude structure (moist tropics positive near the surface). It is a departure from the selection's own mean, not a bias against an independent reference. atmPrf (dry) and wetPf2 (moist) temperature are never averaged together.

J. Maps of a Diagnostic (Statistics tab)

Any per-profile diagnostic of the Compare tab's relationship plot (gravity-wave energy, tropopause height, foF2, TEC, ...) can be averaged in latitude-longitude cells, with its zonal mean, median and standard deviation by latitude band. For the gravity-wave energy the layer and background filter can be chosen (section F). One or two days of data leave most cells with a handful of profiles: read the zonal mean first.

K. Comparison with Radiosondes (Statistics tab)

Each good atmPrf or wetPf2 profile is paired with the nearest radiosonde of NOAA's Integrated Global Radiosonde Archive (IGRA v2) launched within the chosen distance (great circle to the launch point) and time. Both profiles are compared on geopotential height: the sonde's reported height, interpolated in ln p between reported levels; wetPf2's own gph; or atmPrf's height converted with the normal-gravity formula of Mahoney (2001). The sonde's vapour pressure comes from its dew point (or relative humidity) with Bolton's saturation formula, and its refractivity from $N = 77.6\,P/T + 3.73\times10^5\,e/T^2$. Differences are occultation minus sonde: temperature in K, refractivity in percent of the sonde value, specific humidity in g/kg (only where the sonde is warmer than -40 °C); the plot shows their mean and standard deviation in height bins, each pair counted once per bin. atmPrf temperature is the dry temperature, so below about 8-10 km its difference measures the water vapour. Refractivity from radio occultation is known to be biased low near the surface in moist air (3-4 % against RS41 sondes, Ho et al., 2020, 10.3390/rs12244099).

L. Tropopause and Cold Point by Latitude (Statistics tab)

For each good atmPrf or wetPf2 profile of the selection, the WMO lapse-rate tropopause is the lowest level above 500 hPa where the lapse rate $-dT/dz$ falls to 2 K/km or less and its mean to every level within the next 2 km stays at or below 2 K/km; on atmPrf's dry temperature only levels colder than 235 K are considered, because lower down water vapour lowers the dry temperature's lapse rate. The cold point is the coldest level between 10 and 30 km: a tropopause in the tropics only, while in the winter extratropics it lies in the stratosphere. Each latitude band gives the mean with its 95 % confidence interval (Student's t, profiles taken as independent), median, standard deviation and count; bands with fewer than the chosen minimum are blank. With atmPrf in the selection, CDAAC's own WMO tropopause from the same files (attributes trhwmo, trtwmo) is averaged beside them; on a wetPf2 selection those columns are left out. Polar bands can only come from PAZ or KOMPSAT-5 profiles: COSMIC-2 stays within about 45 degrees of the equator.

M. Weather Models at the Same Occultations (Statistics tab)

For every occultation CDAAC publishes the ECMWF fields colocated along its path (echPrf, about 135 levels, from the two ECMWF fields named in its attributes Model_File_1 and Model_File_2) and the NCEP fields interpolated the same way (avnPrf, about 50 levels). Both carry the occultation's file stamp, so they pair exactly with its atmPrf and wetPf2. RO minus model is computed occultation by occultation on a common height grid (refractivity interpolated in log space), in percent of the model value for refractivity and in K for temperature, with the mean, ± 1 standard deviation and the 95 % confidence interval of the mean. wetPf2's 1D-Var takes the echPrf profile of the same occultation as its first guess (attribute fgsPrf), so its RO minus ECMWF temperature is the retrieval's departure from its own first guess, not an independent comparison. Bending angle is compared with ECMWF only (avnPrf has none), by impact height: the simulated bending angle of a model level at height $z$ with refractivity $N$ belongs to the ray whose tangent point is there, with impact height $a = n\,(R_c + z) - R_c$, $n = 1 + 10^{-6}N$ and $R_c$ the occultation's radius of curvature (atmPrf attribute rfict); atmPrf's own Impact_height follows the same relation.

echPrf also carries ECMWF's ducting diagnosis of the occultation (ductid: 0 none, 1 elevated, 2 or 3 at the surface; zsr, the centre of the super-refraction layer). Where the refractivity gradient is below about -157 N-units/km rays are trapped, the Abel inversion has no unique solution and the retrieved refractivity is too low below the duct, by up to about 10 % (Sokolovskiy, 2003, 10.1029/2002RS002728), while its gradient stays above the critical value (Xie et al., 2012, 10.5194/acp-12-903-2012): a retrieved profile cannot show the duct itself. The two ducting views compare RO with ECMWF for the occultations with and without an ECMWF duct, and around the duct by height relative to zsr. On 952 COSMIC-2 and PAZ occultations of 2026, ECMWF diagnosed a duct at 183; there RO minus ECMWF was -4.2 % at 1 km (-1.4 % without a duct) and -8.4 % just below the duct centre.

The three-cornered hat estimates the error variance of each of three datasets $X$, $Y$, $Z$ of the same quantity from the variances of their differences, assuming their errors are independent:

$$\sigma_X^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\left[\mathrm{var}(X - Y) + \mathrm{var}(X - Z) - \mathrm{var}(Y - Z)\right]$$

and likewise for $Y$ and $Z$ (Rieckh et al., 2021, 10.1175/JTECH-D-20-0209.1). Here $X$ is the RO refractivity (atmPrf), $Y$ ECMWF and $Z$ NCEP, in percent of the mean of the three, with 95 % intervals from bootstrap resamples of the occultations. Correlated errors bias the estimates: with error covariances $c$, the RO estimate becomes $\sigma_{RO}^2 - c_{RO,EC} - c_{RO,NC} + c_{EC,NC}$. Both models assimilate COSMIC-2 and largely the same other observations, so neither term is zero; a negative variance means the assumptions fail at that height.

N. COSMIC-2 and Other Missions (Statistics tab)

CDAAC processes PAZ (a sun-synchronous polar orbit) and KOMPSAT-5 into the same atmPrf, wetPf2, echPrf and avnPrf files as COSMIC-2. They are downloaded through their own streams on the Get data tab, kept in their own folders and datasets, and told apart by the satellite code in the file names (C2E1-C2E6, PAZ1, KOM5). Once one of them is on disk, the Explore satellite filter offers "COSMIC-2, all satellites", so that other statistics can leave it out; the weather-model and tropopause captions say when a selection mixes missions.

Three estimates of COSMIC-2 minus the other mission are drawn by height. Collocated: each profile of the other mission is paired with the COSMIC-2 profile nearest in $D_n = 0.75\,|\Delta t|/T + 0.25\,d/d_{max}$ within $d_{max}$ and $T$ (300 km and 3 h by default; the weights of Rieckh et al., 2021, appendix A), each COSMIC-2 profile used once. Minus ECMWF's difference: each pair's difference less the difference between the ECMWF profiles (echPrf) of its two occultations, $(x_{C2} - e_{C2}) - (x_o - e_o)$, which removes the part of the mismatch in place and time that the model resolves (Gilpin et al., 2018, 10.5194/amt-11-2567-2018). Through ECMWF: $\overline{x_{C2} - e} - \overline{x_o - e}$ over the profiles of each mission within the latitudes both cover, with no collocation. On 14 whole days of 2026 (817 PAZ pairs) COSMIC-2 minus PAZ temperature was within about 0.1 K from 2 to 27 km, and at 28-30 km +0.19 to +0.28 K collocated, +0.13 to +0.21 K minus ECMWF's difference and +0.05 to +0.12 K through ECMWF, alike for COSMIC-2's GPS, GLONASS and Galileo occultations: a small real difference, at heights where radio occultation records from different processing are known to differ more (Steiner et al., 2020, 10.5194/amt-13-2547-2020).

4. Profile-Plot Processing (on-screen only)

The Profiles tab can filter and smooth what is drawn. These settings change the plot only: exported data are the archive values and derived fields, and the export provenance says so.

  1. Quality Control: the "Good quality only" filter keeps profiles whose CDAAC bad flag is 0.
  2. Outlier removal (applied once to the whole profile, so it removes values far from the profile's overall distribution, not local spikes on a steep profile):
    • Sigma Clipping: values more than 3 standard deviations from the profile mean.
    • MAD: values more than 3 × 1.4826 median absolute deviations from the median.
    • IQR: values outside $[Q_1 - 3\cdot\text{IQR}, Q_3 + 3\cdot\text{IQR}]$.
    • Percentile: values below the 5th or above the 95th percentile.
  3. Smoothing over a window of 5 levels:
    • Moving Average: uniform running mean.
    • Gaussian: Gaussian kernel with a standard deviation of 5/3 levels.
    • Median: running median, robust to single spikes.
    • Savitzky-Golay: local quadratic fit.
  4. Error bars: the running scatter of the values about their 5-level running mean (so a smooth vertical gradient does not count as uncertainty), as a standard deviation or as the standard error of the running mean. This estimates level-to-level noise, not the retrieval uncertainty.

Updates

A downloaded build looks for a newer release once a week and downloads it in the background (Settings > Updates: turn it off, or Check now). The download is checked against the SHA-256 digest published with the release, and the new build is installed the next time you start the app: only the app folder is replaced, and your downloads, exports, index and settings are kept. The first time the new build starts, a panel shows what is new and lists the settings that are new (with their defaults), changed, no longer allowed or no longer used; closing it changes nothing.

5. Data Provenance and Attribution

Exported CSV files start with a commented header (software version, products and processing streams, number of granules and time range, data citation). netCDF global attributes and JSON exports carry the full provenance block, which adds every input granule file name and the interactive plot settings at export time (marked as not applied). Figure files carry no metadata.
